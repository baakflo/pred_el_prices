Hub-height wind spread vs LEAR error — the registered re-test (final)

Setup. Same design as the 2023-era study, on the era with the physically right variables: 100 m wind (hub height) and ssrd (de-accumulated), from the ECMWF ENS 00Z run of D−1 (leakage-safe). Daily MAE of LEAR(364, academic exog), run lear-de-20260811-074202. Full 6-var span: 2024-03-20..2026-07-29, n=862 days (limited by the LEAR forecast end; ENS archive reaches 2026-08-11).

Pre-registered prediction (2026-08-11, before computation): partial Spearman (hub-height wind spread | wind mean) > +0.15.

Verdict: prediction REFUTED — the partial correlation is exactly zero (−0.002, p=0.95). Daily Germany-mean hub-height ensemble spread carries no incremental signal about LEAR's error beyond plain windiness, replicating the 2023-era null at the physically correct altitude. Even the raw rank correlation vanished on the full span (+0.026 vs +0.164 at the interim look, n=441) — the interim "promising trend" was sample noise, a useful reminder that significant-looking interim p-values (5.5e-04!) do not survive optional stopping. The quartile profile is flat-to-inverted. ssrd spread stays weakly negative (seasonal confounding, as flagged at the interim). The spread-as-heteroscedasticity-driver hypothesis is dead at daily national aggregation for the linear era.

The added year (2025-06..2026-07) includes the record Rhine drought and eclipse-adjacent scarcity pricing — days where LEAR's errors were driven by fuel/hydrology regime factors invisible to wind ensembles. Whatever thin wind-spread signal existed in the first 441 days did not generalize.

What survives for the distributional phase

  • Daily DE-mean spread is out as a variance feature at this aggregation. If ensemble information enters the neural phase, it should be hourly and/or regional spread, or member-level features (e.g. spread of implied residual load), tested against distributional scores (CRPS), not point-error correlation.
  • The archive itself is the asset: 1,295 days x 51 members x 6 variables, leakage-safe, fully QA'd — reusable for any ensemble feature design.
