Hub-height wind spread vs LEAR error — the registered re-test

Setup. Same design as the 2023-era study, on the era where the physically right variables exist: 100 m wind (hub height, cubic power-curve region) and ssrd (de-accumulated to per-3 h energy), both from the ECMWF ENS 00Z run of D−1 (leakage-safe). Daily MAE of LEAR(364, academic exog), run lear-de-20260811-074202. Overlap: 2024-03-20..2025-06-04, n=441 days.

Pre-registered prediction (2026-08-11, before computation): partial Spearman (hub-height wind spread | wind mean) > +0.15.

Interim status. The registered design says "once the full archive lands"; this run uses the ~60% of the 6-var era backfilled so far (through 2025-06-04, backfill in progress). To be re-run on the full 2024-03..2026-07 span when the archive completes.

Verdict (interim): prediction not met. The partial Spearman of hub-height wind spread given plain windiness is +0.088 (p=0.065) — positive, unlike the 2023-era zero, but below the registered +0.15 bar and not significant at n=441. The raw rank correlation (+0.164) again mostly reflects windiness itself. The quartile profile is flat in the mean; only the median creeps up in Q4. ssrd spread correlates negatively with error even after controlling for its level — almost certainly seasonal confounding (solar spread scales with the annual insolation cycle; a season control was not part of the registered design), not a genuine "solar uncertainty helps" effect.

Reading and next steps

  • Moving to hub height moved the partial from 0.00 to +0.09 — the right direction, but weak. Daily Germany-mean aggregation remains the prime suspect for washing out timing-error signal.
  • Re-run on the full span when the backfill lands (registered); the threshold stays +0.15.
  • If the full-span partial stays below the bar, spread enters the distributional phase as a candidate feature at most — not as the heteroscedasticity driver the hypothesis wanted.
