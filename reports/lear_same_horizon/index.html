Findings. (1) Our data pipeline beats the benchmark data at matched windows: academic-exog on our data gives MAE 4.407/4.271 at windows 56/84 vs published 4.619/4.555 (≈4-6% better, rMAE 0.482/0.468 vs 0.506/0.499). Same model, same horizon, same feature spec — the gap is the data source (ENTSO-E/SMARD with QA + gap-patching vs the epftoolbox distribution). (2) Window 364 + academic exog is the best config overall (MAE 3.614, rMAE 0.396), beating even the published 1092/1456-day windows — though that particular comparison crosses window sizes. (3) The extended feature split hurts on this early horizon at every window (e.g. 4.103 vs 3.614 at 364). Plausible mechanism: splitting RES into three series grows the feature count ≈60% while 2016-2017 offshore wind and solar were small — extra variance, no signal. Pre-registered expectation: the split should pay off in the post-2021 high-RES regime; the planned window/feature ablation on 2019-2026 tests this.
Caveats. rMAE anchors are computed per-dataset (weekly persistence on each price series), so cross-dataset rMAE is the safer metric than raw MAE; both datasets cover the same DE-AT-LU zone era so MAE is still informative. Day blocks differ by 1-2h (UTC-day convention on our data vs the epftoolbox day convention). sMAPE/RMSE for the published/repro series appear in reports/lear_reproduction/.
